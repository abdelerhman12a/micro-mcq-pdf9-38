Filaria
Filaria are nematodes transmitted by Arthropods.
Members:
1- Adults in lymphatics as in Wuchereria bancrofti & Brugia malayi.
2- Adults in subcutaneous tissue as in Loa loa & Onchocerca volvulus.
3- Adults in serous cavities as in Acanthochilonema perstans& Mansonella
ozzardi.
General characters of Filaria:
▪ Extra-intestinal nematodes of man.
▪ the mode of infection by bite of infected Arthropod.
Inside Arthropod: Larva increases in size without increase in number
(Cyclodevelopmental transmission)
▪ Larvae enter human body through bite wound.
▪ Larvae go to thoracic muscles of insect then to mouth parts.
▪ Infective stage is 3rd stage filariform larva.
▪ Larval stage in human body is microfilaria first larval stage which appears in
blood at day or night (diurnal or nocturnal periodicity) or in skin.
▪ Adults and microfilariae are present in the same person.

Wuchereria bancrofti
Bancroftian Filariasis, Wuchereriasis, Elephantiasis
Geographical distribution:
Present in: Tropical and Subtropical countries, Middle East, Asia and South
America.
Habitat:
Adults live in lymph nodes and lymph vessels especially of lower limbs and genital
organs. Microfilariae present in blood and lymph.
Definitive host: Man.
Vector: Mosquitoes, Culex, Aedes, Anopheles and Mansonia.
Morphology:
Thread like, creamy white worm with cylindrical esophagus (filariform).
Male:
It is about 4cm long, has curved posterior end with 2 spicules.
Female:
It is about 9 cm long, has 2 sets of genitalia- larviparous (viviparous).

Microfilaria:
The diagnostic stage has loose sheath appears at one or both ends, with rounded
anterior end and tapering posterior end, body forms graceful curves, contains
nuclei but tail is free of nuclei about 250 u..
Infective stage:
3rd stage filariform larva.
Mode of infection:
skin penetration by filariform larva via the bite wound of infected mosquito.
Life cycle:
In man
Third stage larva penetrates skin through bite wound of mosquito, it reaches lymph
node and vessels, within 6-12 months it becomes male and female adults. Adult
female lays first stage larva (microfilaria) which passes through wall of lymphatics
to general circulation, appearing in peripheral blood vessels at night (10pm- 2am)
and disappears at day time (nocturnal periodicity).
In mosquito
When mosquito sucks blood from infected patient it takes microfilariae, which
pass to gut then thoracic muscles where it moult twice giving third stage larvae that
reach the proboscis (mouth parts of mosquito) then penetrate Dutton’s membrane
between labium and labellum and enter skin of man through bite wound of
mosquito to reach lymph nodes and vessels.

I- Acute stage:
Inflammatory manifestations, lymphadenitis and lymphangitis:
a- Due to toxic products of living and dead adult worms with secondary infection.
There is fever, headache, vomiting, chills and malaise, for 15 days and repeated

b- Due to immunological reactions. There are enlargement and erythema with

II- Chronic stage:
Obstructive manifestations:

(4) Haematuria and arthritis.

Diagnosis:
● History and Clinical Presentation: it’s relevant especially in endemic areas,
eg. bilateral lymph node enlargement, oedema of both limbs (upper and lower)
and appearance of complications like; chyluria and hydrocele.
● Parasitological Diagnosis:
1- Giemsa stained thick blood film at night (10 pm to 2 am).
2- Provocation test: giving Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) which forces microfilariae
to appear in peripheral blood during day time.
3- Blood concentration technique (Knott’s procedure): 1 ml of anticoagulated
patient’s blood is added to a tube containing 9ml formalin (2%). Examine the
sediment for microfilariae after staining with Giemsa.
4-Detection of microfilariae in chylus urine.
5-Ultrasonography: worms are seen actively moving inside lymphatics (filarial
dance sign).
6-Immunodiagnosis: serological tests and intradermal test.
a- Serological tests:antibody and antigen detection using ELISA.
b- Intradermal test: usingAntigen extracted from dog Filaria to detect the delayed
type hypersensitivity reaction in epidemiological studies.
7-Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

Treatment:
1. Diethylcarbamazine (Hetrazan):it’s the drug of choice:
microfilaricidal agent which is also capable of killing adults.
2. Albendazole:it kills adult worms.
3. Ivermectin:it is only microfilaricidal.
4. Symptomatic treatment:
Antihistaminic and corticosteroids in case of allergic reaction due to DCE (due
to rapid parasite destruction).
5. Surgical treatment: surgical excision of chronic hydrocele or elephantoid
skin after a course of DEC.
Prevention and control
1. Mass treatment: DEC and Ivermectin
2. Mosquito control:
* Use of insecticides and larvicidal.
* Destruction of breeding places.
Occult Filariasis
It occurs as a result of hypersensitivity reaction to microfilarial antigens.
Microfilariae are not found in blood, as they are destroyed by the tissues.
It is manifested by: Massive eosinophilia (30–80%), hepato-splenomegaly,
pulmonary symptoms, dyspnea and asthmatic wheezing. Classical features of
lymphatic filariasis are absent.

Tropical Pulmonary Eosinophilia
- This is a manifestation of occult filariasis which presents with low-grade
fever, loss of weight, and pulmonary symptoms such as dry nocturnal cough,
dyspnea, and asthmatic wheezing.
- Children and young adults are more commonly affected in areas of endemic
filariasis.
- Diagnosed by high level of serum IgE, and marked increase in eosinophil
count. Serological tests detect filarial antibodies. Filarial antigen is strongly
positive, absence of microfilariae in peripheral blood and chest X-ray shows
mottled shadows similar to miliary tuberculosis.
- The condition responds to treatment with Diethylcarbamazine (DEC), which
acts on microfilariae with rapid improvement and reduction of eosinophilic
number.
Brugia malayi
This parasite is similar to W. bancrofti with the following differences.
- Geographical distribution: east and south Asia, notably India, Indonesia,
Malaysia and Thailand.
- Monkeys and cats are reservoir hosts of certain zoophilic strains.
-Vector: Mansoni and may be Anopheles or Aedes
-Microfilaria is the diagnostic stage 177x 230-5 um, sheathed with kinky curves
and the tail has 2 terminal nuclei and has either nocturnal or non periodicity.
Adults live in lymphatics of upper limb more than lower limb.
Elephatiasis is restricted to area below the knee or elbow
