Balantidium coli
● B. coli is the largest protozoan human
parasite. It is present mainly in Central and
South America, as well as Central Asian
countries.
Life cycle and transmission:
DH: Man.
RH: Pig is the primary reservoir host. Other
mammalians, like monkeys may act as
reservoir hosts. Human infection is usually
acquired from pigs.
Habitat: Large intestine.
Diagnostic stage: Cyst or trophozoite. Life cycle of Balantidium coli.
Infective stage: The cyst.
Mode(s) of infection:
1. Swallowing the cyst in contaminated food or water.
2. External autoinfection (through anal- hand infection) may occur.

Clinical picture
a- Many cases are asymptomatic carriers. The carriers are responsible for spreading
the infection.
b- Acute cases: There’s dysentery with the passage of blood and mucous in the
stools.
c- Chronic cases: Patients have periods of diarrhea alternating with constipation.
d- Complications: In severe cases, appendicitis, perforation of intestine, peritonitis,
and dehydration may occur.
Diagnosis:
- Detection of cyst or trophozoite in stools samples.
Treatment:
- Tetracycline.
- Metronidazole (Flagyl).
Prevention and Control:
- Avoid contact with pig excreta and treatment of infected pigs.
- Safe water supply and sanitary sewage disposal.
- Proper personal hygienic conditions e.g. hand washing before meals and
after defecation.

Giardia lamblia (Giardia intestinalis)
Geographical distribution: Worldwide. Giardia is one of the most common
parasites incriminated in water-borne outbreaks, especially children.
Life cycle and transmission:
- Definitive host: Man.
- Reservoir hosts: Many animals (e.g. dogs, rodents, monkeys). Giardia is
considered one of the most known zoonotic diseases.
- Habitat: Small intestine
(duodenum and jejunum). The
parasite sometimes inhabits the
gall bladder and biliary tract.
- Diagnostic stage: Cyst or
trophozoite.
- Infective stage: Mature
quadri-nucleated cyst. Life cycle of Giardia
l Giardia.
- Mode(s) of infection:
1. Swallowing the cyst in contaminated food or water.
2. External autoinfection (hand-to-mouth transmission) may occur.
3. Person-to-person transmission occurs especially in nurseries, male homosexuals,
mentally ill persons, and school children. Giardia is considered one of the
nosocomial (hospital-transmitted) infections.

4. Mechanical transmission by flies.
- The trophozoite is the active vegetative, dividing form that colonizes the proximal
intestine and never invades the intestinal wall, grows on bile salts resulting in
impairment of fat absorption and digestion (steatorrhea).
- The cyst is the dormant infective form and the only form that can survive outside
the human body.
- Excystation is the dissolving of the outer cyst wall to give rise to trophozoite.
- Encystation is the final stage of the life cycle in which trophozoite withdraws its
flagellae and turns into a cyst.
Clinical Picture
The prepatent period is usually two weeks.
- Many cases are asymptomatic passing cysts in feces.
Acute giardiasis: Sudden onset of explosive watery, foul-smelling diarrhea. Fat
malabsorption (steatorrhea) and flatulence are common. The stools is loose, bulky,
fatty, frothy but without blood. Severe symptoms in immunocompromised
patients are persistent diarrhea (steatorrhea), hypoproteinemia, fat-soluble vitamin
deficiency, lactose intolerance, weight loss, biliary colic, and jaundice.
- Chronic giardiasis: Recurrent attacks of diarrhea alternating with constipation.
- Malabsorption is characteristic of giardiasis, mainly steatorrhea (excessive loss of
fat in stool). This may lead to weight loss and debilitation.
- Occasionally, the gall bladder may be involved causing biliary colic and jaundice.
N.B. The main hazard of Giardia is functional, as it causes marked impairment in
digestion and absorption of food, particularly fat substances.

Diagnosis:
1- Stools examination:
● Macroscopy: Fecal specimens containing G. lamblia may have an offensive odor,
are pale in color and are fatty.
● Microscopy: Detection of cyst or trophozoite in the stools sample by direct smear,
eosin and iodine smears, and concentration methods.
● N.B. Repeated stools examination for three times is recommended as the parasite is
intermittently shed.
2- Detection of the parasite antigen in the stools (coproantigen) by ELISA
immunochromatographic strip tests and indirect immunofluorescent tests (IIF)
using monoclonal antibodies.
3- Serodiagnosis: Antibodies to Giardia are detected by IFA and
ELISA.
4- Molecular diagnosis (PCR).
5- Entero-test (string test): Aspiration of duodenal fluid to detect Enterotest.
the diagnostic stages.
Treatment:
- Metronidazole (Flagyl): 250 mg tablet (three times daily) for 5-10 days.
Children receive 5mg/kg three times daily for five days.
- Tinidazole (Fasigyn): 2gm (4 tablets) as a single oral dose.
- Treatment should be repeated after 7-10 days to avoid autoinfection.
Prevention and Control:
- Personal cleanliness and hygiene.
- Careful washing of fruits and vegetables.
- Safe water supply.

Trichomoniasis
Trichomonads are a group of flagellated protozoa that may infect humans.
Trichomonous vaginalis is the only pathogenic one of them.
Geographical distribution: worldwide more common in developed countries.
Higher prevalence among persons with multiple sexual partners
Final host: man
In the female, lower genital tract(vulva and vagina ) and the male urethra and
prostate.
Exit (diagnostic) stage: Trophozoite (no cyst stage).
Transmission: Man is the only host.
Infective stage: Trophozoite.
Mode of infection: By direct contact during sexual intercourse, rarely may happen
through contaminated towels. Neonates may be infected from mothers during birth
process.
Morphology and life cycle:
The trophozoite is pear shaped with single nucleus and four anterior flagella, an
undulating membrane, and axostyle extending in the midline to the posterior end .it
divides by simple longitudinal binary fission.

Clinical manifestations:
In female:
Vaginitis with a purulent foul smelling discharge is the prominent symptom . It can
be accompanied by vulvar erythema,pruritis . punctate haemorrages of the cervix
may result in strawberry -like appearance . Abdominal pain, dysuria and
dyspareunia may occur.
In male:
The infection is frequently asymptomatic; occasionally, urethritis,
epididymitis,dysuria and prostatitis can occur.
Diagnosis:
-Detecting actively motile organisms in wet smears from vaginal, urethral and
urine or prostatic secretions with rapid jerky movements.
-Fixation and staining of the specimen by Giemsa.
-Direct immunofluorescent antibody staining (more sensitive) .
