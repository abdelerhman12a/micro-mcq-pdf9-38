Mycoplasma
Characteristic features
1. Mycoplasmas are bacteria without cell wall. The lack of a cell wall renders
these organisms:
▪ Resistant to antibiotics which inhibit cell wall synthesis (e.g., beta-lactam
antibiotics).
▪ Unstainable by the Gram stain.
▪ Variable in shape (pleomorphic).
2. Their contents are enveloped by a cell membrane.
3. They are the only bacteria that contain sterol (in the form of cholesterol) in the
cell membrane.
4. They are the smallest bacteria that can be grown on cell-free media.
5. They require serum-enriched medium containing cholesterol. After several
days of incubation, mycoplasmas produce minute colonies which have a

Chlamydia
Characteristic features
1. Chlamydia is a genus of very small (250-400 nm) obligatory intracellular
bacteria. They lack the ability to produce sufficient energy to grow
independently and, therefore, can grow only inside host cells.
2. In the laboratory, they can be grown on tissue culture.
3. Their cell wall structure resembles that of Gram-negative bacteria but lacks
typical peptidoglycan layer.
4. Gram stain is not useful, the organism can readily be stained by Giemsa stain.
5. Chlamydia have a unique biphasic life cycle.
N.B.: Although chlamydia are similar to viruses in being small obligate
intracellular organisms, they are considered bacteria.

Life cycle: There are two morphological forms of Chlamydia
1. Elementary bodies (EBs): They are small, extracellular, metabolically
inert particles. They represent the infectious form.
2. Reticulate bodies (RBs): They are larger, intracellular, metabolically
active particles. They represent the replicating form.
N.B.: The site of replication appears as an intra-cytoplasmic inclusion body,
which can be stained and visualized microscopically.
Chlamydiae that infect humans are divided into three species:
1. Chlamydia trachomatis
2. Chlamydophila pneumoniae.
3. Chlamydophila psittaci.

1-Chlamydia trachomatis
Diseases
I. Genital tract infections: These are sexually transmitted diseases.
1- Nongonococcal urethritis:
It is caused by serotypes D-K.
Males present with urethritis, which may progress to epididymitis, prostatitis or
proctitis.
Females may present with urethritis and/or cervicitis, which may progress to
salpingitis and pelvic inflammatory disease (PID).
The condition may be complicated by infertility or ectopic pregnancy.
2- Lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV):
▪ It is caused by serotypes L1, L2 or L3.
▪ It manifests as an initial genital papule accompanied by suppurative
inguinal lymphadenopathy.
II. Ocular infections:
1- Trachoma:
▪ It is caused by serotypes A, B or C.
▪ Transmission occurs from eye to eye by fingers, fomites and flies mostly
during hot and dry climates.
▪ Trachoma manifests as chronic keratoconjunctivitis with scarring which
may lead to blindness.
2- Inclusion conjunctivitis:
▪ It is caused by serotypes D-K.
▪ It is the most common cause of neonatal conjunctivitis.
▪ Transmission occurs:
In neonates: during passage through an infected birth canal
In adults: due to transfer of organisms from the genitals to the eye (as an
autoinfection) or through a vehicle (e.g., swimming pools).

III. Neonatal pneumonia:
• It is caused by serotypes D-K.
• Infection occurs during passage through an infected birth canal.

Laboratory diagnosis
1. Specimens are taken from urethra, cervix, or conjunctiva according to site of
infection.
2. Direct detection
▪ Microscopic examination of Giemsa-stained smears for detection of the
intracytoplasmic basophilic inclusion bodies.
▪ Antigen detection by immunofluorescence or by ELISA.
▪ Nucleic acid detection by DNA probes or PCR.
3. Cultivation and identification: Specimens are inoculated onto tissue culture.
Intra-cytoplasmic inclusion bodies can be detected by Giemsa stain or
immunofluorescence.
4. Serological tests are not helpful except in lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV)
infection.
5. Frei skin test: It is like tuberculin test. It is rarely used for diagnosis of LGV
infection.

Treatment and prevention
•The antibiotic of choice is erythromycin or tetracycline in adults.
•Erythromycin is recommended for neonates and pregnant women because of the
effect of tetracycline on teeth and bones.
•Chlamydiae are not sensitive to ß-lactam antibiotics due to lack of typical
peptidoglycan layer.

- This organism is transmitted from person to person by aerosols.
- It causes atypical pneumonia.

Rickettsiae
General Properties & Morphology
▪ Small, pleomorphic, Gram-negative bacilli or coccobacilli.
▪ Obligate intracellular parasites; rely on the host cell for energy (ATP).
▪ Stains poorly with conventional Gram stain; best visualized using Giemsa
stain.
▪ Cannot grow on cell-free artificial nutrient media but can grow in (yolk sac
of embryonated eggs, tissue culture, or experimental animals).

Transmission & Pathogenesis
▪ Transmitted via arthropod vectors (ticks, fleas, lice, mites) through bites
or contamination of skin wound by organism during scratching the skin or
crushing the arthropod at bite site
▪ Target Cells: Proliferates within vascular endothelial cells of small blood
vessels.
▪ Mechanisms of Damage: Intracellular multiplication → endothelial cell
necrosis → widespread microvascular vasculitis and thrombosis →
petechial/purpuric skin rash, end-organ ischemia, and Disseminated
Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).

Clinical Manifestations
▪ Classic Clinical Triad: High persistent fever, severe headache, and
characteristic skin rash.
▪ Severe Cases: Encephalitis, delirium, non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema,
and multi-organ failure.

Laboratory Diagnosis
1. PCR: to detect rickettsial DNA in blood or biopsy specimens (e. g., from
skin rash).
2. Serological test: for detection of anti-rickettsial antibodies as well as
rickettsial antigen in biopsy specimen.

Treatment & Prevention
▪ Drug of Choice: Tetracycline for all age groups
▪ Prevention: Vector control, personal hygiene, application of insect
repellents, and delousing campaigns

Coxiella burnetii
General properties:
1-It has endospore-like structure form.
2-Resistant to heat, disinfectant, UV and drying: spores may contaminate milk
products so pasteurization temperature should be raised to greater than 60˚C
3- Non-arthropod transmission: The spores remain viable in dried tick faeces.
These spores are aerosolized and when inhaled causes human disease
(pneumonia).

Disease: Q Fever
Mode of transmission: a zoonotic disease (cattle, sheep and goats)
▪ Inhalation of dust or aerosols contaminated by bacteria from birth canal,
feces, urine or blood during animal handling.
▪ Handling of infected animals or their discharge.
▪ Ingestion of milk from infected animals.

Clinical presentation
▪ Acute Q fever: influenza-like symptoms and/or atypical pneumonia with
hepatitis and no rash.
▪ Chronic Q fever: presented by subacute infective endocarditis

Treatment: doxycycline is the drug of choice.
Prevention
a) Adequate pasteurization of milk.
b) Formalin- killed Coxiella vaccine: to high risk groups (veterinarians,
shepherds, abattoir workers and laboratory personnel).
