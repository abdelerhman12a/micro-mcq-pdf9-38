Loa Loa
(African eye worm)
Geographical distribution: Central and West Africa
Morphology
Adults are similar to W. bancrofti but the cuticle is covered with irregular tubercles
except at the head and tail.
Male: 3.5 cm x 0.35 mm with unequal spicules.
Female: 4cm x 0.5mm with vulva situated at 2.5mm from anterior end.
Microfilaria: 250 x 8μ with tight sheath and kinky curves. Tail end is full of
nuclei. It has diurnal periodicity.

Habitat: Subcutaneous tissues of man and can be seen migrating under the
conjunctiva.

Definitive host: Man.
Intermediate host (Vector): Chrysops (big blood sucking fly).
Infective stage: Infective 3rd stage filariform larva.
Diagnostic stage: Microfilaria in blood.
Life cycle:
The infective 3rd stage larvae penetrate skin through Chrysops bite. Adult worms
develop in subcutaneous tissue and mate to give birth to microfilariae. They will
enter the circulation till taken up by Chrysops during blood meal. Inside the fly

gut, they develop into infective larvae and migrate to mouth parts exactly as what
happens in case of W. bancrofti.

Clinical picture and pathogenesis
The disease caused is called Loaiasis:
Manifestations vary from asymptomatic or mild to life-threatening.
1- Eye lesions: Adults can be seen under the conjunctiva or under the skin of the
bridge of the nose when passing from one eye to the other. This may cause
transient conjunctivitis.
2- Calabar swellings: These transient subcutaneous swellings result from allergic
host reaction to the worm antigens when they remain in one spot for a short time.
They are mostly observed on hands, wrists, forearms and ankles as painless, non pitting hard swellings reaching hen's egg size.
3- Pruritus is a common feature during creeping of the adult worm under the skin.
Fatigue and arthralgia may also be present.
4- Serious CNS, renal and cardiac complications may occur.

Diagnosis
1- Clinical picture of calabar swelling or subconjunctival worm crossing the eye.
2- Identification of microfilariae in blood samples taken during daytime.
3- High eosinophilia (only suggestive of parasitic infection).
4- Immunodiagnosis is of limited value due to cross reaction with other filarial
species.
PCR assay is a sure diagnostic test for microfilaraemia.

Treatment
1- Chemotherapy: DEC and Ivermectin as in W. bancrofti with steroids and
antihistamines to avoid allergic reactions.
2- Surgical removal of adults from under the conjunctiva.

Prevention and control
1- Vector control by use of insecticides at the breeding places and repellents to
reduce the risk of bites.
2- Mass treatment of patients.
3- Prophylaxis of travelers to endemic areas by weekly dose of 300 mg DEC.

Onchocerca volvulus
(Blinding filariasis or River blindness)
Name : Oncho- = tumour – like nodule., Cerca = circular in shape Volvulus =
containing convoluted worms.

Disease : Onchocerciasis – River blindness.
Black flies that transmit disease can breed only on the edges of rapidly flowing
rivers & water streams; so, disease transmission usually occurs at these regions.

Geographical distribution: West & Central Africa;Yemen; Sudan; Saudi
Arabia & Central America.

Habitat: Subcutaneous tissues (inside subcutaneous nodules).
Definitive host: Man.
Intermediate host (Vector): Blood sucking fly, Simulium (Black fly).

• Immune response to dead microfilaria in skin & eye.

Parasite factor:

**Migration of microfilaria from nodules on the face and scalp.

Diagnosis
1- Clinical manifestations.
2- Identification of microfilaria in bloodless skin snip.
3- Detection of adult worms in the nodules.
• Examination of nodules after surgical removal.
• Aspiration of nodules.
• Ultrasonography.
4-Corneo-scleral punch (microfilaria)
5-Mazzotti test: 50 mg DEC initiates intense prurites in few hours due to
microfilarial death. Corticosteroids are given if serious complications occur.
6- Mazzotti patch test (more safe) : DEC patch is applied to skin , local itching in
positive cases (due to death of microfilaria at the site of patch only)
7-ELISA : to detect specific antibodies in serum.
8-PCR: to detect microfilarial DNA in skin snips.

Treatment
1- Surgical removal of nodules.
2- Ivermectin 150 u/kg eliminates the microfilaria of skin and suppresses their
release from adult females for several months. Thus, re-treatment may be
necessary
3- Steroids and antihistaminic to avoid allergic reaction.
4-DEC is not used because it produces severe allergic reaction.

Prevention and control
1-Mass treatment
2-vector control
3-Using of repellent and nets to avoid fly bite.

Dracunculus medinensis
● Other names: Medina worm – Guinea worm – Dragon worm.
● Geographical distribution: Areas with dry climate where wells are
used as water supply (Saudi Arabia, Yemen, West Africa & India).
● Habitat: Subcutaneous tissue (usually of leg near the ankle).
● Life cycle & morphology:
- The adult female worm (100 cm) lives in the subcutaneous tissue near the
ankle & releases toxins that produce local necrosis & ulceration of the
covering skin.
- When becomes in contact with water, a loop of uterus protrudes from
the worm anterior end & emerges from the skin ulcer → the uterine
loop ruptures releasing comma – shaped rhabditiform larvae in water.
- The comma - shaped larvae are then swallowed by Cyclops (the
intermediate host) where they develop into filariform larvae (the
infective Stage).
- Man is infected by swallowing Cyclops (in water) containing filariform
larvae.
After ingestion, the filariform larvae penetrate intestinal mucosa → migrate
through the retroperitoneal space to reach the subcutaneous tissue (usually
near the ankle) within several months.
- During this migration, filariform larvae develop into male & female
worms.
- After fertilization, male worms (about 3 cm in length) die and absorbed.

 Clinical picture :

1- Systemic allergic manifestations due to secretion of toxic materials
(one week before appearance of female worm under skin) erythema
urticarial rash – pruritus – vomiting – diarrhea –dizziness – fever
asthma .
2- Local skin lesion (after appearance of female worm under skin as a
convoluted cord – like swelling): Red papule that becomes blister
then the covering skin is lost (ulcer)…. (Papule  Blister  Ulcer).
3- Complications :
(a) 2ry bacterial infection of ulcer  Cellulitis – Arthritis of ankle
joint.
(b) Calcification of worm (when failed to produce a skin ulcer and lay
its larvae, it dies and becomes calcified).
 Diagnosis :
1- Clinical diagnosis :
(a) Outline of gravid female under the skin.
(b) Local skin lesions (papule  blister  ulcer).
2- Detection of comma – shaped larvae (by immersing leg in cold water).
3- Plain X- ray : for calcified worms.
4- High eosinophilia.
5- Serology.
 Treatment :
1- Forced extraction of worm: the emerging tip of worm is tied on a small
stick then rolled slowly (few centimeters daily) to avoid breaking of
worm that may cause anaphylaxis.
